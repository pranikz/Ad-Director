# Locale: Middle East and Africa (GCC first, plus Egypt, the Levant, Nigeria, South Africa, Kenya)

Read `international.md` first, then this file, before Phase 1 on a brief for these markets. The Gulf (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman) gets the most detail; the African notes are a starting point.

## 1. What works here
Gulf audiences reward **family, generosity, heritage and national pride**, and Ramadan is the biggest advertising season of the year. Egyptian humour travels across the Arab world. Describe these archetypes; never copy a real ad or its lines:
| Archetype | Feel | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Ramadan family film** | A 60–180 s story about generosity, reunion, forgiveness or neighbours, built around the iftar table, released as Ramadan starts; the product appears gently | Telecom, banks, FMCG, food delivery, retail, airlines |
| **Nostalgia and heritage** | Childhood in the old neighbourhood, grandparents, the old souq, traditional crafts and coffee, against today's city | Banks, telcos, real estate, national days |
| **National momentum (Saudi Arabia)** | Young Saudis building things: founders, women in new jobs, new sport and entertainment, delivered with energy and pride | National days, banks, telcos, government and semi-government brands |
| **Family comedy** | Sitcom energy: a big family gathering, the aunt who knows everything, cousins competing; Egyptian-style comedy works region-wide | FMCG, food, telecom, apps |
| **Expat life (UAE, Qatar)** | The multicultural workplace or building, money sent home, several communities' festivals in one compound | Remittances, telecom, banking, retail |
| **Hospitality** | Gahwa (Arabic coffee) and dates in the majlis, welcoming the guest, the host's pride | Food and beverage, hospitality, banks |
| **Football fever** | The café screen, the five-a-side under floodlights, a family split between clubs | Soft drinks, telecom, snacks, delivery |
| **Big-energy comedy (Nigeria)** | Loud, quick, Nollywood-flavoured situational comedy with Pidgin wordplay | Telecom, FMCG, fintech, betting (where allowed) |
| **Neighbourhood warmth (South Africa)** | Township or suburb humour, ubuntu (community), code-switching across languages | Telecom, banks, retail, FMCG |

## 2. Moments and calendar
- **Ramadan** moves about 11 days earlier each year (it starts in early February in 2027). It is the peak advertising and TV season: viewing peaks after iftar, and the tone is family, generosity and faith, handled respectfully. Then **Eid al-Fitr**. **Eid al-Adha** comes with the Hajj, and the Islamic New Year follows.
- **Saudi Arabia:** Founding Day (22 February), National Day (23 September), Riyadh Season and other city seasons (the cooler months). The weekend is Friday–Saturday.
- **UAE:** National Day ("Eid Al Etihad", 2 December), Flag Day (November), Commemoration Day (30 November, solemn and never commercial), the Dubai Shopping Festival (December–January). The weekend is Saturday–Sunday.
- **The rest of the GCC:** each state has its own National Day (Qatar's is 18 December). Check the date and its protocol.
- **Retail:** White Friday (the region's name for Black Friday), 11.11 and back to school (late August).
- **Seasons:** summer is extreme heat and travel season, so people are in malls, cars and homes. October to April is outdoor season: desert camping, terraces, events.
- **Sport:** Formula 1 (Bahrain, Jeddah, Qatar, Abu Dhabi), the Saudi Pro League, the Africa Cup of Nations, and the 2034 FIFA World Cup in Saudi Arabia.
- **Expat festivals in the UAE and Qatar:** Diwali, Onam, Christmas, Lunar New Year.
- **Egypt:** the Ramadan TV drama season, Sham el-Nessim, Coptic Christmas (7 January).
- **Africa:** "Detty December" in Nigeria (the diaspora comes home and parties), Nigerian Independence Day (1 October), Heritage Day in South Africa (24 September, also "Braai Day"), rugby season, Kenya's national days.

## 3. Language and dialogue
| Market | Write dialogue in | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman | Gulf Arabic (Khaleeji) for nationals; English for expat stories | Bilingual Arabic and English supers are standard |
| Saudi Arabia | Saudi Arabic (Najdi for Riyadh, Hejazi for Jeddah) | Arabic first. Financial ads must be in Arabic (SAMA) |
| Egypt | Egyptian Arabic | The most widely understood dialect in the Arab world |
| Levant | Levantine Arabic | — |
| Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia | Darija plus French | Darija is hard for Gulf viewers; don't use it for pan-Arab work |
| Nigeria | Nigerian English, Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa | Code-switching is natural |
| South Africa | English, isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho and others | Characters switch languages mid-sentence; write it that way |
| Kenya | English, Swahili, Sheng (Nairobi street slang) | — |

- **Modern Standard Arabic** suits formal VO and legal lines. Dialogue goes in the dialect of the cast and place: an Emirati family doesn't speak Egyptian.
- **Arabic is right to left:** see `international.md` section 3. Gulf ads often use Western digits; follow the brand.
- **Seedance Arabic and African-language lip sync is untested.** Transcript QA is mandatory. The fallback is action-led films with an MSA or local VO.
- **Expat targeting:** Hindi, Urdu, Malayalam or Tagalog versions for campaigns aimed at those communities in the Gulf.

## 4. Casting
- **Nationals and expats:** in the UAE and Qatar most residents are expats (South Asian, Filipino, Arab, African, Western). Reflect that in everyday stories, and cast nationals for national-pride and heritage stories.
- **National dress must be exact:** the Emirati kandura with ghutra and agal; the Saudi thobe with the red-and-white shemagh; the Omani dishdasha with kumma or massar; and the Qatari and Kuwaiti variations. Women wear the abaya with a shayla or hijab, some a niqab. In Saudi cities younger women may wear coloured abayas, so follow the brief. Models blend these, so write them out and check them.
- **Modesty for Gulf briefs:** women covered from shoulders to ankles in loose clothes; men not shirtless; no physical affection between men and women. Mixed-gender scenes are fine at work and in families, at a respectful distance.
- **Skin tone and features vary:** Gulf Arabs range from light to deep brown, and there are Afro-Arab communities. Write the tone, with no lightening; colourism exists here too.
- **Family:** extended families, respected elders and hands-on fathers.
- **Africa:** cast specific ethnicities. **Nigeria bans foreign models and voice-over artists in ads aimed at Nigeria** (ARCON, since October 2022), so write Nigerian identities, use a Nigerian voice artist, and confirm with the person how ARCON treats synthetic performers. In South Africa, reflect the Black majority and the Coloured, Indian and White communities, with no poverty-porn township imagery.
- **The models' MENA cliché** is desert, camels, sepia and ancient souqs. Avoid it unless the brief wants heritage.

## 6. Cultural sensitivity (fail QA on any of these)
- **Religion:** no Quranic verses, no mosque interiors or prayer in comic or product contexts, no depictions of prophets, and the call to prayer only with respect, never as a sound effect.
- **No alcohol, pork, gambling, nightclubs or dating.** Avoid dogs inside the home for conservative Gulf audiences.
- **No physical intimacy, revealing clothes, suggestive or mixed-gender dancing.**
- **LGBTQ+ content is prohibited in the GCC.** Cut separate versions where needed.
- **Etiquette:** eat, give and receive with the right hand; never point the soles of the feet at people; shoes off on the majlis carpet.
- **Rulers and national symbols:** never depict real rulers or royals. Treat flags and emblems with respect. The Saudi flag carries the Shahada, so it never appears on products, on the ground, upside down or at half-mast.
- **Ramadan ads:** no daytime eating, drinking or smoking; only iftar and suhoor.
- **Names and maps:** say "Arabian Gulf" in the GCC. Never show maps or borders (Israel and Palestine, Western Sahara and others).
- **Avoid** politics, regional conflicts, magic, witchcraft and horror.
- **Africa:** no poverty imagery, no "Africa is one country", no safari clichés. Balance Christian and Muslim in Nigeria. No apartheid imagery for laughs in South Africa.

## 7. Claims, supers and compliance (check against the brief and current rules)
- **UAE:** the UAE Media Council and its media content standards (respect for Islam and other religions, the state and its symbols, leaders, national identity and social values; nothing harmful to minors). Ads for medicines, health products and medical services need prior approval from the health authority (the federal ministry, or the Dubai or Abu Dhabi health department). Financial ads follow Central Bank rules. Individuals posting promotional content need the **Advertiser Permit** (mandatory since late October 2025), so brands should only work with licensed creators. Prices are shown VAT-inclusive, and sales promotions need emirate-level permits.
- **Saudi Arabia:** the General Authority for Media Regulation (GAMR) sets the content controls, and paid influencer content needs a **Mawthooq** licence. The SFDA approves food, drug, cosmetic and medical-device claims. SAMA's rules for financial ads require Arabic and disclosed risks. Discounts and promotions need Ministry of Commerce approval.
- **Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman:** similar ministry-level media and health approvals. Kuwait and Qatar are more conservative.
- **Across the GCC:** claims are substantiated; disparaging competitors is risky; prices are in local currency.
- **Egypt:** the Supreme Council for Media Regulation; ads in Arabic.
- **Nigeria:** ARCON's Advertising Standards Panel must approve ads before exposure, and the foreign models and voice-over ban applies.
- **South Africa:** the Advertising Regulatory Board's Code of Advertising Practice, the Consumer Protection Act, SAHPRA for health products, and the alcohol industry's code.

## 8. Formats and deliverables
- **TV still matters at Ramadan** (pan-Arab satellite channels), with the long film on YouTube and 30 and 15 s cutdowns for TV.
- **YouTube is huge in Saudi Arabia, and Snapchat is a top platform there.** Make 9:16 with sound and Arabic captions. TikTok and Instagram are strong across the region.
- **Bilingual end cards** (Arabic and English) with an RTL layout and the CTA in both languages.
- **Africa is mobile-first and data-conscious:** short, lighter files, plus WhatsApp-status versions (9:16, about 30 s).
