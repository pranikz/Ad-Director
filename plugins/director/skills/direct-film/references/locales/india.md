# Locale: India (default)

Read this before Phase 1 on any brief for an Indian audience, which is the default. It layers Indian specifics on top of `storytelling.md`, `realism.md` and `qa.md`.

## 1. What works in Indian advertising
Indian audiences reward ads that feel **like their own street, home or family**, and they share the funny or moving ones on WhatsApp. These are the archetypes that keep working (describe them; never copy a real ad or its lines):
| Archetype | Feel | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Wordplay / pun** | One everyday Hindi or English word flips into the product sense, in deadpan slice-of-life | Banking, telecom, FMCG, apps (e.g. the worked example's "light/lite" set) |
| **Mohalla / small business** | Kirana, chaiwala, tailor, chemist, tiffin service, auto driver: real customers being clever | Current accounts, UPI, B2B, logistics, credit |
| **Family dynamics** | Joint family, saas-bahu, papa–beti, siblings, grandparents online: warmth with a comic edge | FMCG, insurance, telecom, ed-tech, auto |
| **Middle-class aspiration** | First car, first flat, kid's coaching fees, a parent's pride | BFSI, real estate, auto, education |
| **Jugaad** | A clever fix to an everyday constraint, which the product makes effortless | Apps, tools, appliances |
| **Festive emotional** | One small gesture around a festival; the product is in the gesture, not the speech | Gifting, jewellery, food, payments |
| **Absurd exaggeration** | The benefit taken to a ridiculous, very Indian extreme | Adhesives, paints, snacks, gum |
| **Cricket** | Gully cricket, IPL fever, "bad light", the six, the umpire uncle | Anything during IPL or World Cup windows |

## 2. Moments and calendar
- **Festivals:** Diwali, Dhanteras, Holi, Eid, Christmas, Navratri and Garba, Durga Puja, Ganesh Chaturthi, Onam, Pongal, Makar Sankranti, Lohri, Baisakhi, Bihu, Raksha Bandhan, Karva Chauth, Chhath.
- **Seasons and civic moments:** wedding season (Nov–Feb), monsoon, exams and results, back to school, Independence and Republic Day, financial year-end (March) for BFSI.
- **Sport:** IPL (about Mar–May), cricket World Cups, India–Pakistan matches.
- **Rule:** use a festival only if the brief allows it. Get the rituals, clothes, food and décor exactly right for *that* region's version of the festival (Durga Puja ≠ Navratri ≠ Dussehra).

## 3. Language, scripts and dialogue
| Market | Language | Write dialogue in |
|---|---|---|
| North / central / national | Hindi, **Hinglish** | Devanagari. English words as people say them: "करंट अकाउंट", "पेमेंट"; acronyms in Latin ("FD", "UPI") |
| Maharashtra | Marathi / Hindi | Devanagari |
| Gujarat | Gujarati | Gujarati script |
| Punjab | Punjabi | Gurmukhi |
| West Bengal | Bengali | Bengali script |
| Tamil Nadu | Tamil | Tamil script |
| Andhra Pradesh, Telangana | Telugu | Telugu script |
| Karnataka | Kannada | Kannada script |
| Kerala | Malayalam | Malayalam script |
| Odisha | Odia | Odia script |
| Assam | Assamese | Bengali-Assamese script |

- **Proven:** Seedance 2.5 Hindi sync dialogue is close to word-perfect (8/8 films on the worked example). Other Indian languages are **untested**, so treat transcript QA as mandatory. If a language fails, fall back to a film without dialogue or with minimal Hindi, plus a native-language VO and supers on the end card.
- **Code-mixing is natural.** Mix as the character would: a Bengaluru techie mixes more English, a Lucknow chemist less.
- **Address terms set the relationship:** bhaiya, didi, kaka, tai, anna, akka, dada, chacha, uncle-aunty, beta, saab, madam-ji. Use the right one for the region.
- **South markets:** prefer the native language over Hindi. A Hindi-only ad for Tamil Nadu or Karnataka can land badly.
- **Numbers drift.** Spell them phonetically (छे, not छह) and gloss them in English in the prompt.
- **Regional versions:** a re-shoot with local cast and language beats a dub. If the budget only allows a dub, avoid close-up lip-sync in the master.

## 4. Casting India (built different, with dignity)
- **Vary everything:** region, community, skin tone (deep brown, dusky, wheatish, fair), age from 19 to 75, body type, gender of the hero, and occupation.
- **Colourism is a known model bias.** Video models lighten Indian skin and "westernise" features. Write the skin tone explicitly in every brief and add EXTRA RULE "keep every skin tone exactly as described — no skin lightening".
- **Wardrobe is regional and real:**
  - lungi or mundu, a vest with a towel on the shoulder;
  - nauvari or a Bengali drape, cotton saree, salwar-kameez;
  - kurta-pyjama, pathani suit, safari suit, half-sleeve shirts with pens;
  - patka or turban, monkey cap, scooter helmet;
  - sacred thread, kara, bindi, sindoor, vibhuti stripes, skullcap, hijab, as authentic to the character, never as a costume joke.
- **Specific is better than generic:** "64, big-boned Maharashtrian tai, nine-yard cotton saree tucked at the waist, flour on her hands", not "Indian woman".
- **Never reproduce real people.** No celebrity faces or voices. Indian courts protect celebrity personality rights, and imitating a star is a legal risk as well as slop.

- **Mumbai:** chawl courtyards, a bus-depot tapri, a local-train platform, a Dadar flower market.
- **Pune:** a wada kitchen.
- **Surat:** a textile gaddi shop.
- **Lucknow:** a 24-hour chemist.
- **Kolkata:** a para and a tram stop.
- **Hyderabad:** Old City lanes, an Irani café.
- **Chennai:** a mess hall.
- **Bengaluru:** a darshini, an apartment society.
- **Kerala:** a coastal fishing village.
- **Punjab:** a farm.
- **A small-town kirana.**

Generate the place as a **people-free plate** with honest clutter: unbranded boxes, tangled wires, steel tumblers, calendars without readable text, tube lights.

## 6. Cultural sensitivity (fail QA on any of these)
- Deities, scripture or places of worship are never shown in comic or product contexts. No footwear near a puja or altar.
- Food fits the community and region. Beef or pork, alcohol and smoking need an explicit brief OK; surrogate alcohol or tobacco advertising is not allowed.
- No caste markers or caste humour. Communities and regions are never the punchline.
- Gender: no harmful stereotypes, so chores are not only women's work and men are not shown as useless at home (ASCI's guidelines on harmful gender stereotypes). Women as decision-makers and business owners is normal, not a twist.
- Children are shown playing, never working, always fully dressed, never in danger.
- Darker skin is never shown as a problem to fix (ASCI's guidance on skin-lightening products).

## 7. Claims, supers and compliance (check against the brief and the current rules)
- **ASCI code and CCPA misleading-ads guidelines:** claims must be truthful and substantiated. Disclaimers must not contradict the claim, should be legible, should be on screen long enough to read, and should be **in the same language as the claim**. Check current ASCI guidance for size and duration.
- **Banking (RBI-regulated):** state rates as "up to" with "*T&C apply. Interest rates are subject to periodic changes." when the brief says so. Never promise a guaranteed return the bank didn't state.
- **Mutual funds (SEBI/AMFI):** the standard market-risk disclaimer is mandatory and must be both shown and spoken.
- **Insurance (IRDAI):** the solicitation line, registration number and product UIN, as the brief supplies them.
- **Food and health:** no unsubstantiated health or nutrition claims (FSSAI), and nothing aimed at children for HFSS foods.
- **Endorsements:** don't imply that a real person or body endorses the product.
- Put exact figures only on the end card, copied **character for character** from the brief, with the asterisk and disclaimer. Dialogue keeps it soft ("कमा भी रहा है"). If the brief's VO and supers disagree (₹50,000 vs ₹25,000), flag it to the person; don't pick one.

## 8. Formats and deliverables
- **TV and OTT:** 10, 15, 20 and 30 s in 16:9. IPL in-match spots are often 10 s.
- **YouTube:** 6 s bumpers, plus 15–30 s.
- **Instagram Reels and Shorts:** 9:16, 15–30 s, with the hook in the first 2 s and supers sized for mobile.
- **WhatsApp status:** 9:16, about 30 s maximum.
- **Regional versions:** language, cast and supers swapped per market. Cutdowns go 30 → 15 → 10 → 6.
- **End card:** the brand's sonic logo or jingle if they have one, an English and/or regional-language super, and the CTA ("Open your account today", "Download the app", "Visit your nearest branch").

## 9. Worked example
`../../examples/lantern-bank-lite/` holds 8 Hindi comedy spots for a bank current account (a fictional Lantern Bank Lite Current Account: an Auto Sweep FD at up to 6% p.a.), built on the light/lite pun. It includes casting across Sikh, Gujarati, Tamil, Marathi, Bengali and UP characters, gully cricket with kids, ASCI-style supers, and an end card with a festive CTA.
