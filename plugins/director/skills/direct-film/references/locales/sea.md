# Locale: Southeast Asia (Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, the Philippines, Vietnam)

Read `international.md` first, then this file, before Phase 1 on a brief for Southeast Asia.

## 1. What works here
Southeast Asian audiences reward **big emotion and big laughs**, and they share long branded films on YouTube and Facebook. Festivals that bring families home are the tentpoles. Describe these archetypes; never copy a real ad or its lines:
| Archetype | Feel | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Thai tearjerker** | A 2–4 min story of quiet, selfless kindness or sacrifice, with a reveal at the end that makes you cry | Life insurance, telecom, banks, pharmacies, healthcare |
| **Thai absurdist comedy** | A bizarre premise played completely straight, with deadpan acting and punchy sound design | Fast food, snacks, drinks, insurance, apps |
| **Festive homecoming (MY, SG, ID)** | Balik kampung and mudik journeys home for Hari Raya and Lebaran, the Lunar New Year reunion dinner, Deepavali open houses: generations reconciling, neighbours of different races sharing the day | Fuel, telecom, banks, FMCG, e-commerce |
| **Kilig love story (Philippines)** | Romance, family and "hugot" (achingly emotional) lines; Valentine's series of short films | Fast food, telecom, FMCG |
| **OFW homecoming (Philippines)** | An overseas Filipino worker coming home, the balikbayan box, the family that waited | Remittances, telecom, banks, airlines |
| **Shopping-festival spectacle** | Bright colours, an earworm jingle and a dance for 9.9, 11.11 and 12.12 (the jingle must be original) | E-commerce, wallets, delivery |
| **Kiasu comedy (Singapore)** | Gently mocking the fear of missing out: queues, chope-ing seats, over-planning; natural Singlish | Banks, telcos, food delivery, public campaigns |
| **Tết homecoming (Vietnam)** | The trip home before Tết, the family meal, lucky money, a parent who waits | Drinks, banks, FMCG, ride-hailing |
| **Ngabuburit comedy (Indonesia)** | Killing time before breaking the fast in Ramadan, with young friends and their schemes | Telecom, food and drink, e-commerce |

## 2. Moments and calendar
- **Lunar New Year** (January–February): Singapore and Malaysia, Tết in Vietnam (the biggest holiday of the year), Imlek in Indonesia, and Chinese-Thai families in Thailand.
- **Ramadan and Hari Raya Aidilfitri, or Lebaran** (Indonesia, Malaysia, Brunei, Singapore): the mudik and balik kampung exodus home, and open houses. Also Hari Raya Haji.
- **Deepavali and Thaipusam** (Malaysia, Singapore).
- **Thailand:** Songkran (Thai New Year and water festival, mid April), Loy Krathong (November).
- **Philippines:** the Christmas season starts in September (the "Ber months") and runs to January; also Holy Week, Undas (1 November) and Valentine's Day.
- **Vietnam:** Tết, the Mid-Autumn Festival (a children's festival), and Vietnamese Women's Day (20 October), a big gifting day alongside 8 March.
- **National Days:** Singapore (9 August), Malaysia (Merdeka on 31 August, Malaysia Day on 16 September), Indonesia (17 August).
- **Shopping festivals:** the double-date sales (9.9, 10.10, 11.11, 12.12) and payday sales.
- **Also:** the Mid-Autumn Festival (mooncakes) across Chinese communities, and Vesak.
- **Sport:** English Premier League fandom, badminton, the SEA Games, MotoGP and Muay Thai.

## 3. Language and dialogue
| Market | Write dialogue in | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Singapore | Singapore English (Singlish where natural), Mandarin, Malay, Tamil | National campaigns often run in all four |
| Malaysia | Bahasa Malaysia, English (Manglish), Mandarin (Cantonese or Hokkien flavour), Tamil | Malay versions matter for broadcast |
| Indonesia | Bahasa Indonesia; Jakarta slang (bahasa gaul) for youth | Javanese or Sundanese colour for regional briefs |
| Thailand | Thai, in Thai script | Thai has no spaces between words, so overlay line breaks need a Thai-aware font and a check. Isan dialect for the northeast |
| Philippines | Taglish (Tagalog–English code-switching) by default | Cebuano or Bisaya for the Visayas and Mindanao |
| Vietnam | Vietnamese | Northern (Hanoi) or Southern (Saigon) accent to match the place. Check that fonts render every tone mark |

- **Vietnam's language rules:** ads must be in Vietnamese. Foreign-language text may be no more than three-quarters the size of the Vietnamese and sits below it, and in audiovisual ads the Vietnamese comes first.
- **Seedance lip sync in these languages is untested.** Transcript QA is mandatory, with the usual fallback (action-led film, native VO, supers).

## 4. Casting
- **Name the community:** Malay, Chinese (Hokkien, Cantonese or Hakka heritage), and Indian (mostly Tamil) in Malaysia and Singapore; Javanese, Sundanese, Batak, Minang, Chinese-Indonesian and Papuan in Indonesia; Central Thai, Thai-Chinese and Isan in Thailand; Tagalog, Visayan and Moro in the Philippines; Kinh Vietnamese in Vietnam. Models default to generic "East Asian", so write the specifics.
- **Colourism is strong in the region,** and models lighten skin too. Write every skin tone, add the no-lightening EXTRA RULE, and never make fair skin the route to success.
- **Multi-racial casting** is expected in Malaysian and Singaporean national campaigns. **Never have one actor play another race:** a 2019 Singapore "brownface" ad caused a national backlash, and ASAS added guidance on race sensitivities.
- **Wardrobe:** the tudung or hijab for Muslim women characters in Malaysia and Indonesia when the brief fits, with modest dress; baju Melayu and songkok for Raya; batik in Indonesia; the barong in the Philippines; the áo dài in Vietnam; Thai school and office uniforms; monks in saffron (with the rules in section 6).
- **Families are multi-generational,** and elders are respected.

## 5. Places (name the city and the spot)
- **Singapore:** an HDB void deck, a hawker centre, a kopitiam, an MRT carriage, a Tiong Bahru shophouse.
- **Malaysia:** a mamak stall, a kampung house on stilts, a Penang shophouse, a KL condo, a pasar malam (night market).
- **Indonesia:** a Jakarta gang (alley), a warung, a kos (boarding-house room), an angkot minibus, a Yogyakarta becak lane.
- **Thailand:** a Bangkok soi with street food, a motorbike-taxi stand, a Thonburi canal house, an Isan rice farm, a Chiang Mai market.
- **Philippines:** a sari-sari store, a jeepney, a barangay basketball court, a carinderia, a provincial bahay kubo.
- **Vietnam:** a Hanoi street café with plastic stools, a Saigon alley full of motorbikes, a Mekong Delta waterway.
- **Details:** motorbikes, humidity, plastic stools, tiled floors, rice cookers. Traffic drives on the **left** in Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand and on the **right** in the Philippines and Vietnam; models get this wrong, so check it. Plates stay people-free with no readable text.

## 6. Cultural sensitivity (fail QA on any of these)
- **Thailand:** never depict, reference or parody the monarchy (lèse-majesté is a serious crime). Buddha images are never decoration, never placed low and never comic. Women don't touch monks, and monks aren't shown eating after midday. The head is sacred (don't touch heads); never point feet at people or Buddha images.
- **Malaysia:** the "3R" (race, religion, royalty) are never the punchline. Halal norms apply: no pork or alcohol near Muslim characters, and halal claims need JAKIM certification. Avoid Muslim characters touching dogs. LGBTQ+ content is restricted.
- **Indonesia:** halal, religious harmony and Pancasila; LGBTQ+ content is restricted on broadcast, and the broadcast regulator is strict on dress and intimacy.
- **Singapore:** racial and religious harmony is protected by law. No blackface or brownface, and no community as the joke.
- **Philippines:** Catholic imagery with respect, and national heroes are not for parody (a diaper ad spoofing a national hero lost its clearance).
- **Vietnam:** sovereignty matters. Never show maps (South China Sea lines have got films and products banned), and avoid war imagery, Party symbols and national leaders.
- **Gambling** is illegal in most of the region; **alcohol** advertising is heavily restricted in Thailand (eased in 2025 under conditions, so check), Malaysia and Indonesia.

## 7. Claims, supers and compliance (check against the brief and current rules)
- **Singapore:** ASAS administers the Singapore Code of Advertising Practice (legal, decent, honest, truthful). There is no pre-vetting, but rulings are published. Broadcast ads also follow IMDA's TV and radio advertising code. Health products fall under HSA, and ads for the least healthy grade of sugary drinks (Nutri-Grade) are banned.
- **Malaysia:** the Malaysian Code of Advertising Practice (ASA Malaysia) and the Communications and Multimedia Content Code. Medicine and health-product ads need Medicine Advertisements Board approval, shown by its number. The old "Made in Malaysia" rule for TV commercials required local production and talent; enforcement is unclear, so check with the broadcaster.
- **Indonesia:** Etika Pariwara Indonesia (the industry code) and the broadcast regulator's standards. Over-the-counter drug ads need BPOM approval before publication, in Bahasa Indonesia (rules rewritten in April 2026). Food and cosmetic claims also go through BPOM, and halal claims need certification.
- **Thailand:** the Thai FDA pre-approves food, drug, cosmetic and medical-device ad claims; the Consumer Protection Board covers misleading ads; the NBTC covers broadcast; and the alcohol rules are strict.
- **Philippines:** the Ad Standards Council screens and clears ads before they run (TV, radio, cinema, digital and outdoor); broadcasters need the clearance. Food supplements carry the FDA's current mandatory disclaimer, and gambling ads need ASC and PAGCOR vetting.
- **Vietnam:** the Law on Advertising sets the language rules above. Health-related ads need Ministry of Health confirmation first, foreign advertisers work through local agencies, and content that offends sovereignty or tradition is banned.
- **Across the region:** whitening claims are scrutinised. Financial ads follow the central banks' and securities regulators' risk-disclosure rules (MAS, BNM, OJK and others).

## 8. Formats and deliverables
- **Mobile-first:** TikTok, YouTube and Facebook (especially the Philippines and Vietnam), Instagram, in-app video on Shopee and Lazada, LINE in Thailand and Zalo in Vietnam.
- **Long branded films** (2–5 min) on YouTube for festivals, with 30, 15 and 6 s cutdowns. TV is still strong in the Philippines, Indonesia and Thailand.
- **Multi-language supers** for Singapore and Malaysia, Vietnamese-first supers in Vietnam, and Thai line breaks checked frame by frame.
- **End card:** the CTA in the market's language, app-store badges, and the regulator's approval number where the product needs one (health, medicine).
