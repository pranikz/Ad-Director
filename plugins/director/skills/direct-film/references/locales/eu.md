# Locale: Europe (EU, plus Switzerland and Norway)

Read `international.md` first, then this file, before Phase 1 on a brief for European markets. The UK and Ireland are in `us-uk.md`.

## 1. What works in Europe
Europe is many markets that share some rules. A pan-European buy rewards ideas that **work without words**, while national campaigns reward local wit. Describe these archetypes; never copy a real ad or its lines:
| Archetype | Feel | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Wordless visual gag** | Physical comedy or a visual twist with no dialogue, so one master runs in 20 languages with only the supers swapped | Pan-EU FMCG, auto, travel, apps |
| **Christmas tearjerker** | A national holiday short film about kindness, loneliness or a village sharing luck; Spain, Germany and France each have the tradition | Supermarkets, retail, telecom, lotteries (where allowed) |
| **Engineered understatement (DACH)** | Precise, product-proof, dry wit; the product works and nobody makes a fuss | Auto, appliances, tools, B2B, insurance |
| **French cinematic** | Mood, irony, a philosophical voice, beautiful light | Luxury, fashion, fragrance, food, auto |
| **Nordic deadpan** | Understated humour, social realism, equal roles at home, nature close by | Grocery, telecom, energy, banks |
| **Italian family table** | Loud warmth, three generations, food as love | Food, coffee, auto, telecom |
| **Dutch directness** | Straight talk about price and value, with self-mocking thrift | Retail, telecom, insurance, energy |
| **Supermarket fable** | An animated or hybrid holiday fable told through a small creature or object | Grocers and retailers at Christmas |

## 2. Moments and calendar
- **Christmas season:** Advent and Christmas markets; St Nicholas (6 December in Germany, Austria and Belgium; Sinterklaas on 5 December in the Netherlands, where blackface "Piet" imagery must never appear); Spain's Christmas lottery (22 December) and Three Kings (6 January, Spain's main gift day); Santa Lucia (13 December, Sweden).
- **Carnival and spring:** Karneval and Fasching (February), Easter, King's Day (27 April, Netherlands), Eurovision (May).
- **Summer:** Midsummer (June, the Nordics), Bastille Day (14 July, France), the Tour de France (July), Ferragosto (15 August, Italy). August is holiday month in France, Italy and Spain, so expect low attention.
- **Autumn:** la rentrée and back to school (September), Oktoberfest (September–October, Munich), Black Friday (now Europe-wide).
- **Sales:** France has fixed legal sales periods ("les soldes", winter and summer).
- **Sport:** the Champions League, national football leagues and the Euros, cycling, Formula 1, and winter sports in the Alps and the Nordics.
- **Ramadan and Eid** matter for the large Muslim communities in France, Germany, the Netherlands and Belgium.

## 3. Language and dialogue
- **Version per country, not per language.** Austrian German isn't German German. Belgium needs French and Dutch (Flemish), Switzerland German, French and Italian. Spain may need Catalan, Basque or Galician regionally. Latin-American Spanish doesn't suit Spain, and Brazilian Portuguese doesn't suit Portugal.
- **Dubbing and subtitling habits differ:** Germany, France, Italy and Spain dub; the Nordics, the Netherlands and Portugal subtitle. Ads use a local VO everywhere. For pan-EU masters prefer wordless or VO-led structures; otherwise re-generate the dialogue film per language (`international.md` section 3).
- **Formal or informal address** (du or Sie, tu or vous, tú or usted): follow the brand's tone of voice, and keep it consistent across the set.
- **France (Toubon law):** advertising must be in French. A foreign-language slogan needs a French translation that is as legible or audible as the original.
- **Supers:** German, French and Spanish run longer than English, so leave room. Use the decimal comma (6,5 %), the local currency format (9,99 € in many countries) and day-month dates.

| Market | Write dialogue in |
|---|---|
| Germany, Austria, German-speaking Switzerland | German (Austrian or Swiss variants where the cast is local) |
| France, Wallonia, French-speaking Switzerland | French |
| Spain | Castilian Spanish (regional languages on request) |
| Italy, Ticino | Italian |
| Netherlands, Flanders | Dutch (Flemish for Flanders) |
| Nordics | Swedish, Danish, Norwegian, Finnish (never one for all) |
| Poland, Czechia, Portugal, Greece and others | The national language |

## 4. Casting
- **Cast the real local mix:** Turkish-German families in Berlin, North-African French in Marseille, Surinamese-Dutch in Amsterdam, Polish workers in a Dutch greenhouse, Romanian and Moldovan communities in Italy. Avoid "generic Europe": blond, thin and twenty-something.
- **Europe is older than its ads.** Cast people over 50 as leads, not only as grandparents.
- **Equality at home** is the expectation in the Nordics and much of western Europe: men doing childcare and housework is normal, not a joke.
- **Same-sex couples** are ordinary in western and northern Europe. Parts of central and eastern Europe are more conservative, and Hungary restricts LGBTQ+ content that minors can see, ads included. Check per market.
- **Wardrobe and setting cues:** write them per country (a Bavarian Tracht only for Oktoberfest-type briefs, Breton stripes only if the brief wants the cliché). Models drift to Paris and the Eiffel Tower for any "European" prompt.

## 5. Places (name the city and the spot)
- **Germany:** a Berlin Späti, a Kreuzberg Hinterhof courtyard, a Hamburg harbour fish market, a Munich allotment garden (Schrebergarten).
- **France:** a banlieue boulangerie, a Lyon bouchon, Marseille's Vieux-Port, a Provence village market.
- **Spain:** a Madrid neighbourhood bar, a Seville patio, a Barcelona Eixample flat with a balcony.
- **Italy:** a Naples Quartieri Spagnoli alley with laundry overhead, a Milan tram, a Sicilian beach town.
- **Benelux:** an Amsterdam canal house with steep stairs, a Rotterdam bike lane, a Brussels frietkot.
- **Nordics:** a Copenhagen cargo-bike street, a Stockholm allotment, a Helsinki sauna.
- **Elsewhere:** a Warsaw housing estate (blokowisko), a Lisbon tram hill, an Athens periptero kiosk.
- **Hardware:** European plugs, left-hand-drive cars driving on the right, metric signage, bikes everywhere. Plates stay people-free with no readable text.

## 6. Cultural sensitivity (fail QA on any of these)
- **Nazi symbols and Holocaust imagery** are illegal in Germany and Austria and offensive everywhere. No WWII jokes.
- **Blackface,** including Sinterklaas "Piet" imagery.
- **Religion:** parodies of Christian imagery have been ruled offensive in France and Italy; France's laïcité makes religious content sensitive.
- **Conflicts, borders and separatism:** Russia and Ukraine, the Balkans, Cyprus, Catalonia and the Basque Country. Never show maps.
- **Colonial imagery** (Belgium and the Congo, France and Algeria, and others).
- **Roma and migrant stereotypes.**
- **Sexism and objectification** are regulated more actively than in the US (France, Spain, the Nordics).
- **Children:** Sweden bans TV ads aimed at children under 12, and EU rules protect minors across audiovisual media.

## 7. Claims, supers and compliance (check against the brief and national rules)
**EU-wide**
- **Unfair Commercial Practices Directive:** no misleading or aggressive practices, plus a blacklist of practices that are always unfair. It is enforced nationally by consumer authorities, courts and, in Germany, by competitors and associations through cease-and-desist letters (Abmahnung).
- **Self-regulators** under the EASA umbrella: ARPP (France; TV ads need its opinion before broadcast), the Deutscher Werberat (Germany), Autocontrol (Spain, which offers copy advice), IAP (Italy), the Stichting Reclame Code (Netherlands) and JEP (Belgium).
- **Comparative advertising** is allowed when objective, verifiable and like for like, with no denigration.
- **Green claims:** the Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition rules have applied since 27 September 2026. Generic environmental claims ("eco-friendly", "green", "climate-friendly") are banned unless the trader can show recognised excellent environmental performance. "Climate neutral" or "reduced impact" claims based on offsetting are banned, as are whole-product claims that only cover one aspect. Only certified sustainability labels are allowed. Green imagery (leaves, an all-green palette) can mislead too.
- **AI Act, Article 50:** since 2 August 2026, realistic AI-generated people and scenes must be disclosed (`international.md` section 4).
- **Food:** only authorised health claims (the Nutrition and Health Claims Regulation).
- **Prices:** a "was" price in a sale must be the lowest price of the previous 30 days.
- **Gambling** is banned or heavily restricted across much of the EU (Italy, Spain, Belgium and the Netherlands among them). Assume no unless the brief has local clearance.

**Country specifics**
- **France:** advertising in French (Toubon). The Loi Évin limits alcohol ads to factual product information with a health warning: no TV, no lifestyle or emotion. Ads for processed food and drinks carry the mandatory health message. Car ads carry an active-mobility message and the #SeDéplacerMoinsPolluer hashtag. Commercial photos with retouched bodies are labelled "photographie retouchée", and influencer content needs "image retouchée" or "image virtuelle" labels for edited or AI-made faces and bodies.
- **Germany:** competitors enforce claims fast. Health and medicinal claims are tightly regulated, and "test winner" claims must cite the source and date.
- **Nordics:** Norway requires a label on ads with retouched bodies and bans alcohol advertising almost entirely; Sweden and Finland restrict it heavily. Sweden bans TV ads aimed at under-12s.
- **Hungary:** restricts LGBTQ+ content accessible to minors.

## 8. Formats and deliverables
- **TV:** 30, 20, 15 and 10 s, with 20 s common in Germany. Loudness to EBU R128. France needs ARPP clearance before broadcast.
- **Cinema advertising** is still significant in many countries.
- **Social:** as in `international.md` section 7.
- **Per-country deliverables:** the language version (generated or VO), supers and legal lines in that language, the mandatory national messages (France: food and car messages), the AI-disclosure super, and a textless master for any later versions.
